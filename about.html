About

Nichole Elana

I am a first-generation Colombian-American born by the Pacific, where ocean meets memory.

— Who I Am

Raised by hands that understood care as ceremony.

My roots stretch from the Afro-Colombian rhythms of the Pacific coast to the Andes, the Cherokee lands of the Southeast, and the folk traditions of Swedish-German lineage.

Their medicine lived in flower baths and simmering pots, in remedies whispered in kitchens, in songs carried to the water, and in the quiet ways people learned to sustain life together.

This is the ancestral language I remember, and the one I am still learning to speak.

— My Philosophy

Life is relational.

We are shaped through our relationships — with our bodies, our families, our communities, our ancestors, the land beneath our feet, and the living world that sustains us. Wellness, belonging, creativity, grief, nourishment, and healing do not exist separately from one another. They are threads of the same tapestry.

This work invites a return to relationship.

  • Relationship with the body and its rhythms.
  • Relationship with food and the stories it carries.
  • Relationship with beauty, pleasure, creativity, and expression.
  • Relationship with ancestry and inheritance.
  • Relationship with place.
  • Relationship with community.
  • Relationship with the seasons and cycles that shape a life.
  • Relationship with the living world itself.

Food, song, story, beauty, ritual, and gathering are not separate practices, but different ways of entering into conversation with the world around us. Because healing is not separate from living — it emerges through the ways we learn to care for what has been entrusted to us.

— Teachers & Lineage

My first teachers were the hands that raised me.

Long before any formal study, I learned care as ceremony — in kitchens and flower baths, in remedies whispered over pots, in songs carried to the water. My lineage is not a single thread but many woven together: Afro-Colombian, Andean, Cherokee, Swedish-German. Each carries its own memory of how life is tended.

For more than a decade, I have studied herbalism, nourishment, and the ritual arts — the many ways people tend life through seasons of change. That study has been shaped as much by lived experience as by formal training: through my own relationship with womanhood, fertility, illness, ancestry, grief, creativity, community, and becoming.

I carry these teachers with me — the named and the unnamed, the living and the ancestral — and I offer this work in service to what they entrusted to me.

— Why Madre y Agua

A home for the threads I had been gathering all along.

  • Food
  • Flowers
  • Water
  • Story
  • Rhythm
  • Relationship

Madre y Agua first arrived as a dream. Over time it revealed itself as a place where the everyday and the ceremonial meet — devoted to the ritual arts of living in relationship.

To cultivate spaces, practices, and offerings that nourish the relationships that sustain life — toward a world where care is remembered as an art.

— An Invitation

However you arrived here, you are welcome.

Today I continue following the threads between memory and becoming — caring for what we have inherited while cultivating what wishes to emerge. Step in through a consultation, a gathering, a story, or simply a conversation.
